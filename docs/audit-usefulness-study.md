# Audit Report Usefulness Study

Purpose: turn the first cohort of IEP Audit families into evidence that the
report changes what happens at the IEP table. Serves three needs at once,
product refinement, funder and application evidence, and the start of the
outcome dataset.

N is small. Eight families is a qualitative study, not a survey, so the
instrument is short, mostly open, and built to produce usable quotations
rather than percentages.

---

## Wave 1: Report Received

Send 3 to 5 days after the report goes out. Target completion time, 4 minutes.

1. Did you read the whole report, part of it, or none of it yet?
   - All of it / Most of it / Some of it / Not yet

2. Was anything in the report confusing or hard to follow?
   Open text. This is the single most valuable product question in the study.

3. Was there anything in the report you did not already know about your
   child's IEP?
   - Yes, several things / Yes, one or two things / No, it confirmed what I
     already suspected / No, I already knew all of it

4. What is the one finding you are most likely to raise with the school?
   Open text.

5. After reading it, how prepared do you feel for your next IEP meeting
   compared to before?
   - Much more prepared / Somewhat more prepared / About the same / Less
     prepared, it raised more questions than it answered

6. Is there anything you wanted the report to tell you that it did not?
   Open text.

---

## Wave 2: After the Meeting

Send within one week of the family's next IEP or 504 meeting. Ask on intake
when that meeting is scheduled so this can be timed rather than guessed.
Target completion time, 5 minutes.

1. Did you take the report, or anything from it, into the meeting?
   - Yes, I brought the report / Yes, I brought notes from it / No, I read it
     beforehand but brought nothing / No, I did not use it

2. Did you raise any of the findings with the team?
   - Yes / No / The meeting has not happened yet

3. If you raised something, what happened?
   - The team agreed and changed the IEP
   - The team agreed to look into it or follow up
   - The team disagreed
   - The team acknowledged it without committing
   - Open text follow-up, tell us what was said

4. Did anything change in your child's IEP or services as a result?
   Open text. Capture specifics, added minutes, a new goal, an evaluation
   agreed to, a service restored, a placement discussion opened.

5. Did the report change how you spoke or what you asked for in that room?
   Open text. This is the self-advocacy outcome and the heart of the mission,
   so leave room for a long answer.

6. Would you recommend the audit to another parent?
   - Yes / No / Not sure, plus one line on why

7. May we quote you anonymously in our reports to funders and on our website?
   - Yes, anonymously / Yes, with my first name / No

## What to record on every audit from here forward

Capture these at intake and at report delivery so the dataset builds without
a separate research effort. None of it requires extra family burden.

| Field | Captured at | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| State and district | Intake | Enables district-level and state-level pattern claims later |
| Grade level and disability category | Intake | Shows whether findings cluster by category |
| Date of next scheduled IEP meeting | Intake | Times Wave 2 and measures report-to-meeting lead time |
| Request-to-report turnaround in days | Delivery | You already claim a three-day median, so evidence it |
| Count of conditions flagged, by domain | Delivery | The core product metric and the seed of the aggregate dataset |
| Most frequently flagged condition | Delivery | Becomes the headline finding in an annual report |
| Race and household income band | Intake, optional and self-reported | You already state that every family served is Black and half fall below 200 percent FPL, so keep the evidence clean |

---

## How to run it

Send Wave 1 to all eight families now, since several are likely past the
point where their meeting has happened and you can run both waves together
for those. Follow the written survey with a 15-minute phone call offer for
any family willing, because with eight families the calls will teach you more
than the forms will, and a transcript yields quotations a checkbox never does.

Keep it in whatever tool already reaches these families rather than adding a
platform for eight responses.

## What counts as the pilot being proven

The report is ready to stand behind, and the venture moves from piloting to
proving, when three things hold across roughly twenty to thirty families.

1. Wave 1 question 2 stops surfacing new comprehension problems, meaning the
   report reads clearly to parents without a walkthrough.
2. A majority of families reach Wave 1 question 5 at "much more prepared."
3. Wave 2 produces a repeatable pattern of families raising findings in the
   room, whatever the district's answer turns out to be. The outcome you
   control is whether the parent speaks, not whether the district agrees.
