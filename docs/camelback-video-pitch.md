# Camelback Video Pitch, 3 Minutes

File name: `reba_clarke-wedderburn_pitch.mp4`

## Read this before the script

**Do not read this aloud from the screen.** Camelback says outright that they
do not want a polished promotional video, and a founder reading a script has a
particular flat, downward-inflected sound that everyone recognizes. Learn the
four beats and the handful of specific numbers, then talk. Your fifth take
speaking freely will beat your first take reading, every time.

**The timing is tighter than it sounds.** Three minutes at a natural speaking
pace is about 400 words, and that count assumes you never pause. The script
below runs 382 words and lands near 2 minutes 38 seconds, which leaves roughly
20 seconds of room to breathe, restart a sentence, or let the close land.
Founders lose this task by running long and getting cut off inside the vision
section, which is the section that decides whether they advance.

## Beat 1, what the venture is (0:00 to 0:32)

> I'm Reba Clarke-Wedderburn, and I founded EDquity at the Margins.
>
> A child's IEP decides what services that child actually receives, and almost
> no parent has an independent way to know whether it's any good.
>
> So we read it for them, free. A family sends us the document, we evaluate it
> blind against a forty-condition rubric, and we send back a plain-language
> report telling them what's missing and what to say about it at the next
> meeting.

Say "forty-condition rubric" clearly and do not rush past it. That phrase is
the difference between sounding like a helpful person and sounding like
someone who built an instrument.

---

## Beat 2, your why (0:32 to 1:13)

**This is the beat I cannot write for you, and it is the one they weigh most.**
You have about 100 words here, which is your most valuable stretch of the
entire application.

Three things it has to do, in this order:

1. **One specific scene.** A room, a family, a moment you watched something go
   wrong. Not a category of experience, one occasion. Names changed, details
   kept.
2. **What it cost you.** The thing you could not fix from the seat you were
   in. This is the hinge, because it explains why you left a system you had
   spent twenty-one years inside instead of staying to improve it.
3. **The decision.** The moment it stopped being a frustration and became a
   thing you were going to build.

Rules for this beat. Speak in past tense about the scene and present tense
about the decision, because that shift is what makes a story land. Do not
summarize the emotion, describe what happened and let the listener supply it.
Resist the urge to establish your credentials here, since your CV sits
elsewhere in the application and spending this beat on qualifications is the
single most common way founders waste it.

**Shape reference only, replace every word of this:**

> I sat in a meeting where a mother agreed to something she didn't understand,
> because six professionals told her it was best and she had no way to know it
> wasn't. She signed. I knew that document was wrong, and I wasn't in a
> position to say so. I went home and I couldn't let it go, because I had been
> training the teachers on the other side of that table for years, and I
> realized I had never once trained the parent.

That reference runs 80 words, so you have about 20 more to work with. Use them
on the scene rather than on the explanation.

---

## Beat 3, who it is for (1:13 to 1:41)

> We serve families whose children have the disabilities schools treat as
> routine. Learning disabilities, speech, ADHD. Not the complex cases that get
> everyone's attention, but the ones that get processed.
>
> Every family we've served identifies as Black, and half live below twice the
> federal poverty line. They will never pay us anything.
>
> Schools and districts pay, out of federal professional development money
> they're already required to spend.

That last line does heavy lifting in fifteen words. It answers the
sustainability question before anyone has to ask it, and it signals that you
understand where school money actually comes from, which most education
founders do not.

---

## Beat 4, vision and impact (1:41 to 2:32)

> Independent advocates charge a hundred to three hundred dollars an hour. That
> puts a price on knowing your own child's rights, and the families who need it
> most sit underneath it. I want to take the price off.
>
> The audit stays free permanently, and school systems fund it by paying us to
> measure their own IEP quality against the same rubric. Every audit also
> builds something that doesn't exist anywhere in this country, real data on
> IEP quality by district, by race, and by income.
>
> We've served eight families. I'm deliberately not chasing volume yet, because
> I'm proving the report changes what a parent says in that room first. I won't
> sell this to a superintendent until I can show them it works.

Say eight. Do not soften it, do not say "a number of families," and do not skip
it. Following a small honest number immediately with the reason it is small
converts it from a weakness into evidence of judgment, and a reviewer who hears
that will trust every other number you gave them.

---

## Beat 5, close (2:32 to 2:38)

> I want every child to have a "ME" fighting for them. That's the whole thing.

Then stop. No thank you, no contact details, no trailing off. Let it end on the
sentence.

---

## Recording notes

Shoot on a phone, propped at eye level rather than handheld, with a window in
front of you and nothing bright behind you. Sit somewhere quiet and ordinary,
because a kitchen table reads as more honest than a staged office and Camelback
has explicitly said they are not judging production.

Record the whole thing in one take, several times, rather than recording it in
sections and stitching them together. Four or five full runs will get you one
you like, and the good one is usually the third or fourth, once you have
stopped trying to remember the wording.

Time every take. If you land past 2:50, cut words from Beat 4 rather than
speaking faster, because a rushed vision section undoes the calm you built in
the first two beats.

Watch one take back with the sound off. If you look like you are reading, you
are, and you need another pass.

Upload well before the deadline, since Camelback warns that the platform slows
under load, and check that the file name reads exactly
`reba_clarke-wedderburn_pitch.mp4` before you send it.
