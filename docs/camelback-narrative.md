# Camelback Cohort 18, Narrative Source

Working material for the Camelback Fellowship application. The portal reveals
the real prompts after the eligibility quiz, so these are the underlying
blocks, written to be cut down and remapped onto whatever wording appears.

Everything factual here is traceable to something that actually happened. No
number in this document is aspirational.

---

## The framing decision everything else follows from

Camelback invests in the founder before the venture. They back people building
for communities they belong to, and their whole model assumes the venture is
early enough to still be shaped. This has one practical consequence worth
holding onto through every answer:

**Precision beats scale.** Eight audits with a live validation study running is
a stronger application than forty audits with no evidence, because the first
describes a founder who builds on data and the second describes one who
mistakes activity for proof. Do not inflate. Say eight, say what you learned,
say what you are doing about it.

The second framing decision: the venture is the instrument, not the service.
Anyone can offer to help a parent read an IEP. Almost nobody has built a
scored, blind, objective quality rubric with state-level overlays. Lead with
the instrument in every answer about the solution, because the instrument is
the part that scales and the part nobody can copy quickly.

---

## The problem

IDEA made parents equal partners at the IEP table in law. In practice, the
district arrives with evaluators, administrators, and a special education
coordinator who all share a vocabulary and a procedural fluency the parent was
never given. The parent arrives alone.

That asymmetry is not evenly distributed. It falls hardest on Black families,
low-income families, and families who do not speak English at home, and it
compounds against documented racial and economic disparities in how children
get identified, placed, and served.

Independent advocacy exists as a market answer, at roughly $100 to $300 an
hour. That price sets the ceiling on who gets help, and the families who most
need it sit under the ceiling.

Sharpening note: keep the problem statement about the *information asymmetry*
rather than about districts acting in bad faith. Most IEP teams are not
adversaries, they are overloaded, and a solution framed around informing
parents scales to every district. A solution framed around fighting districts
scales to none.

---

## The solution

A free, independent IEP audit. A family sends the document, and it gets
evaluated blind against a 54-condition rubric across six domains, with state
overlays layered on top where state law exceeds the federal floor. Tennessee
is built, and it matters because Tennessee requires the transition plan two
years earlier than IDEA does, which means a 14-year-old in Tennessee passes
every federal transition check while actually being out of compliance.

The family receives a plain-language report naming what is missing, what is
legally inadequate, and what to say about it before the next meeting.

What the venture does not do, deliberately: no representation, no attendance
at IEP meetings. The goal is a parent who can speak for their own child, not a
parent who has swapped dependence on the district for dependence on an
advocate. This constraint is also what makes the model scale, because a
service that requires the founder in the room caps at the founder's calendar.

## The sustainability model

Families never pay. Institutions do. The free audit is the acquisition engine
and the data engine, and every audit run adds a row to a dataset on IEP
quality by district, category, race, and income that currently exists nowhere
else.

Three institutional lines, all built on the same instrument:

1. **The EDquity Leader Fellowship**, $8,000 per school seat, paid from Title
   II-A or IDEA Part B professional development allocations, so it never
   competes with local operating funds. A school submits three redacted IEPs
   monthly, gets them blind-scored against the master rubric, and the cohort
   works the trends together. Status: founding cohort forming.
2. **District professional development contracts**, five sessions built and
   priced, invoiced by purchase order. Status: launching.
3. **Pre-Employment Transition Services**, as a contracted Community
   Rehabilitation Provider with Tennessee Vocational Rehabilitation. Public
   money from the WIOA 15 percent federal set-aside, per student, statewide
   across all 95 counties, serving students 14 to 22 with an IEP or 504 and no
   severity threshold. Status: provider path researched, not yet applied.

Be candid that none of the three is contracted yet, and say why that
sequencing is correct: a district does not buy a rubric, it buys a validated
instrument, so the instrument gets validated first. The usefulness study
finishing is what makes the $8,000 seat defensible, and that is the work
between now and February.

One weakness to fix before submitting rather than defend in the interview: the
Fellowship is currently designed as non-renewable, which caps lifetime value
per school at $8,000 and forces the pipeline to rebuild every year. Name the
post-Fellowship audit subscription as a priced product and the model reads as
recurring revenue instead of a series of one-off sales.

## What the Fellowship unlocks

Answer this concretely. The weakest version is "capital and mentorship." The
strong version names the specific constraint the venture is stuck against.

Candidate constraints, pick the true one:
- The instrument is validated on eight families and needs a study large enough
  to defend in front of a superintendent.
- The institutional sales cycle runs on relationships in district central
  offices, and it is a full-time motion that a solo founder cannot run
  alongside delivery.
- Everything currently depends on one person, and no part of the audit process
  is documented well enough for a second evaluator to run it.

That third one is probably the real answer, and it is the most fundable,
because it is the difference between a practice and a venture.

## The founder story, which I cannot write for you

This is the part Camelback weighs most heavily, and it is the one part of this
document I will not draft from public facts, because inventing a personal
origin for a founder would be both wrong and instantly detectable in an
interview.

Your CV is not the story. Twenty-one years, the doctorate from Lipscomb, the
F.L.O.W. framework, LEAD Public Schools, TNTP, Western Governors, Nashville
Teacher Residency, those establish that you are credible. They do not explain
why this problem and not another one.

Answer these and I will draft it:

1. When did you first watch a family lose something at an IEP table that they
   should have won? What specifically happened, and what did you do or fail to
   do?
2. You wrote "I want every child to have a 'ME' fighting for them." Who was
   the child, or who was the parent, behind that sentence?
3. What did you see from inside the system, in coaching or in licensure work,
   that you could not fix from that seat? The pivot from insider to founder is
   the spine of the story, and the reason you left is the hinge.
4. What is your own family's or your own experience of this? Answer only if
   you want it in the application, and say plainly if you do not, because it
   is legitimate to keep that out.
5. Why now, in 2026, and not five years ago?
