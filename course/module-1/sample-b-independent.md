## Internal design note, not for learners

Fully synthetic, on the same basis as sample A.

**This document inverts sample A's pattern, and that inversion is its whole job.** Sample A meets every compliance condition and fails every substantive one. A learner who takes the wrong lesson from the walkthrough leaves believing compliance passes and substance fails, then applies that shortcut here and gets it backwards.

Compliance 3, substantive 4, total 7 of 10.

| Condition | Score | How it lands |
|---|---|---|
| C1, named assessment, date, score, each area of need | 0 | Math is scored, the reading need is described without an instrument or a number |
| C2, disability effect on the general curriculum | 1 | Specific to this student in this curriculum, not transferable boilerplate |
| C3, observable skill and criterion, every goal | 1 | Solve and write, each with a criterion |
| C4, method and reporting schedule, every goal | 1 | Both goals name a probe and the quarterly schedule |
| C5, every present-levels need has a goal | 0 | The reading comprehension need has no goal |
| S1, quantified baseline in the goal, every goal | 1 | Both goals carry their own from-and-to, traceable to the named probes |
| S2, appropriately ambitious rate of improvement | 0 | The computation goal asks 4 of 10 to 6 of 10 across a full year |
| S3, condition, setting, or materials, every goal | 1 | Reference card and graphic organizer, named in the goals |
| S4, administrable measurement tool, every goal | 1 | Both probes name item count, scoring, and administration setting |
| S5, passes the stranger test as a set | 1 | A new teacher could teach both goals from these two sections |

**S2 is the sharpest item in module 1.** Goal 1 is otherwise exemplary. It carries its condition, its baseline, its target, its consistency requirement, its schedule, and a tool specific enough to replicate. It moves Priya from 4 of 10 to 6 of 10 across thirty-six weeks while she sits at the 8th percentile, which is one additional correct item roughly every eighteen weeks and does not begin to close the gap present levels describes. Every compliance condition it touches is met and it fails Endrew F. on the arithmetic. No monitoring visit in the country would flag it.

The scope rule does real work here. S2 is scored across every goal, so goal 2's defensible target cannot rescue goal 1's, and the condition scores 0 for the document. A learner who averages the two goals will land on 1 and should be able to say why that is wrong: Priya has a goal that expects almost nothing of her, and averaging hides the child that finding is about.

**C1 and C5 are the two compliance failures, and they share a root.** Present levels describes a reading comprehension need in prose without an instrument, a date, or a score, then no goal addresses it. A learner who scores C1 as met because the math data is thorough has missed that the condition applies to each area of need described. Worth watching in the facilitated version, since it is the most common scoring error on this document.

## Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance

**Student strengths.** Priya is a capable verbal reasoner who contributes strong ideas in discussion and works well with peers in structured group tasks. She persists on problems she can begin independently and asks for help appropriately when the task is clear.

**Parent input.** Priya's family reports that she completes math work at home when an adult helps her identify the first step, and that she describes multi-step problems as "too long" before attempting them. They would like her to work independently on grade-level math by the end of the year.

**Academic achievement, mathematics computation.** On the aimsweb Math Computation probe administered October 2, 2026, Priya scored 12 digits correct in two minutes, placing her at the 8th percentile for eighth grade. On a curriculum-based measure of two-step linear equations administered the same week, she solved 4 of 10 items correctly with a calculator available. Errors concentrated in items requiring two operations, where she completed the first step correctly and stopped.

**Academic achievement, mathematics problem solving.** On the district problem-solving measure administered October 3, 2026, Priya scored 2 of 8 on multi-step word problems, correctly identifying the operation in 6 of 8 items while completing the full solution in 2. She did not record her work on any item.

**Academic achievement, reading.** Priya's teachers report that she has difficulty with longer reading passages and appears to lose track of information across paragraphs. She reads grade-level text accurately aloud.

**Functional performance.** Priya arrives prepared, tracks her own assignments in a planner, and submits work on time in all four core classes.

**Effect of disability.** Priya's other health impairment affects sustained attention during multi-step tasks in the general education curriculum. In mathematics this appears as abandoned problems after a correct first step rather than as computational error, so her scores understate what she can do on any single operation. Because she does not record intermediate work, neither she nor her teacher can locate where a solution broke down, which prevents her from correcting her own errors.

## Measurable Annual Goals

**Goal 1, Mathematics Computation.** Given a two-step equation reference card, Priya will solve two-step linear equations, increasing from 4 of 10 to 6 of 10 items correct across 3 of 4 consecutive weekly sessions, evaluated by a 10-item two-step equation probe scored for correct final answers and administered at independent practice level. Progress will be reported quarterly on the district progress report form.

**Goal 2, Mathematics Problem Solving.** Given a four-step problem-solving graphic organizer, Priya will solve multi-step word problems showing all intermediate steps, increasing from 2 of 8 to 6 of 8 items correct across 3 of 4 consecutive weekly sessions, evaluated by an 8-item district problem-solving measure scored with the district four-point work-shown rubric and administered during math class. Progress will be reported quarterly on the district progress report form.
