# Reviewer Key: Sample A, Marcus D., Grade 5

Module 1 guided walkthrough · Edquity at the Margins

**Compliance 5 of 5. Substantive 0 of 5. Total 5 of 10.**

Read the shape before the total. Every compliance indicator is met, every substantive indicator is not. This document would pass a monitoring visit and it does not tell a new teacher what to do on Monday, which is the claim module 1 exists to demonstrate.

## Compliance

**C1. Met.**
Reading names the aimsweb Reading Curriculum-Based Measure, administered September 12, 2026, at 62 words correct per minute with 94% accuracy and the 9th percentile. Comprehension is reported the same date at 5 of 10 questions on the accompanying passage set. Written expression names the district writing rubric, administered September 15, 2026, at 2 of 4 in organization and 2 of 4 in conventions, with an average of 41 words per sample. Every area of need carries an instrument, a date, and a score, so all three parts are satisfied in all three areas.

**C2. Met.**
The statement reads that the disability "affects his progress in the general education curriculum in every subject that requires him to read independently," then names the mechanism: decoding consumes his attention, so he retains less of what he reads than what he hears. That sentence describes this student in this curriculum and could not be transferred to another child with the same eligibility, which is the test.

**C3. Met.**
Read, answer, and write are observable. Each goal carries a criterion: 75 words correct per minute, 70% accuracy, 3 of 4 in organization. Both parts are present in all three goals.

**C4. Met.**
Each goal names a measurement method and states that progress will be reported quarterly on the district progress report form. Both parts appear in every goal.

Score this indicator on whether a method is stated, not on whether the method is any good. Classroom reading assessments is a method, and it is a bad one. Its quality is S4's business, and conflating the two is the most common error on this document.

**C5. Met.**
Present levels describes three areas of need: reading fluency, reading comprehension, and written expression. Goals 1, 2, and 3 address them in that order. No need is left uncovered.

## Substantive

**S1. Not met.**
No goal states a baseline. Goal 1 targets 75 words correct per minute without naming the 62 that present levels reports. Goal 2 targets 70% without naming the 5 of 10. Goal 3 targets 3 of 4 without naming the 2 of 4.

**This is the judgment to slow down on.** A learner who scored this met almost certainly reasoned that the baselines exist in the document, which is true, and C1 gives the document full credit for exactly that. The indicator asks something different: whether the goal itself carries its trajectory. A teacher handed goal 1 alone cannot tell whether 75 words per minute represents a year of hard growth or a number Marcus will pass in October, and the person teaching the goal is often not the person reading present levels. One goal missing the baseline would be enough to mark this not met. Three are missing.

**S2. Not met.**
Compute goal 1 rather than judging it by feel. From 62 to 75 words correct per minute across roughly thirty-six instructional weeks is about 0.36 words per week. Ordinary fifth-grade growth on this measure runs closer to a word per week, so the goal asks for roughly a third of typical growth from a student at the 9th percentile. Present levels puts the benchmark at 110, so the goal closes 13 points of a 48-point gap and leaves Marcus further behind at the end of the year than at the start.

Goal 3 fails on its own terms as well. Present levels documents 41 words per sample against an expectation near 90, and the goal addresses only the organization score while ignoring the length deficit entirely.

Goal 2, moving 50% to 70%, is the one defensible target in the document. One unambitious goal is enough to mark the indicator not met, and there are two.

**S3. Not met.**
No goal names a condition, setting, or materials. Nothing states whether Marcus reads with or without a preview of the passage, whether the comprehension questions are answered orally or in writing, or whether the narrative paragraph is drafted by hand or on a device. Each of those changes what the score means, and each is left to whoever administers the measure.

**S4. Not met.**
Classroom reading assessments, teacher-created comprehension questions, and classroom writing samples. None names what the tool contains, how it is scored, or where it is given, so two teachers measuring the same goal would produce numbers that cannot be compared.

Goal 3 comes closest, because the district writing rubric appears inside the criterion and is a real scoring instrument. The measurement method remains classroom writing samples, which specifies no prompt type, no length, and no conditions, so the indicator is not met.

**S5. Not met.**
Present levels would genuinely orient a new teacher, and the goals then withhold everything that teacher would need: no starting point, no materials, no measure anyone could repeat. A teacher could not tell at the end of a week whether a lesson moved Marcus toward any of these three goals. The document also tells one story in present levels, where written output length is a documented problem, and a different story in the goals, where length is never mentioned.

## What the walkthrough should land

The compliance column is full and the substantive column is empty, and that pattern is not an accident of this document. Compliance asks whether the required text is present, which a template can guarantee. Substance asks whether the text would move a child, which no template can. Marcus has a complete IEP and an unteachable one.
