# Reviewer Key: Sample B, Priya S., Grade 8

Module 1 independent scoring key · Edquity at the Margins

**Compliance 3 of 5. Substantive 4 of 5. Total 7 of 10.**

Sample A met every compliance indicator and failed every substantive one. This document inverts that. A learner who left the walkthrough holding "compliance passes, substance fails" as a rule applies it here and gets seven of ten judgments backwards, which is what a cold document is for.

---

## Compliance

**C1. Not met.**
Mathematics is scored thoroughly. The aimsweb Math Computation probe, administered October 2, 2026, at 12 digits correct in two minutes and the 8th percentile. A curriculum-based measure of two-step equations the same week at 4 of 10. The district problem-solving measure, administered October 3, 2026, at 2 of 8.

Reading is not. Present levels reads that "Priya's teachers report that she has difficulty with longer reading passages and appears to lose track of information across paragraphs." No instrument, no date, no score.

**This is the most-missed judgment on this document.** A learner who scored it met was reading the math data, which is genuinely strong. The indicator applies to each area of need present levels describes, and present levels describes a reading need, so one unscored area leaves the indicator not met. Thoroughness in one area does not cover silence in another.

**C2. Met.**
The statement names sustained attention during multi-step tasks, then specifies how it appears in this curriculum: abandoned problems after a correct first step rather than computational error, so her scores understate what she can do on any single operation. It goes on to explain that because she records no intermediate work, neither she nor her teacher can locate where a solution broke down. That is a mechanism specific to this student.

**C3. Met.**
Solve is observable in both goals. Goal 1 carries 6 of 10 items correct, goal 2 carries 6 of 8. Both parts, both goals.

**C4. Met.**
Goal 1 names a 10-item two-step equation probe and quarterly reporting. Goal 2 names an 8-item district problem-solving measure and quarterly reporting. Both parts, both goals.

**C5. Not met.**
Present levels describes a reading comprehension need. No goal addresses it.

The accommodations list includes text-to-speech for passages longer than two pages, and a learner may reasonably ask whether that covers the need. It does not, and the reason is worth stating precisely: text-to-speech removes a decoding barrier, while present levels describes Priya losing track of information across paragraphs and reading grade-level text accurately aloud. Accurate oral reading means decoding is not the problem. The need is a comprehension skill breakdown, which calls for instruction and therefore a goal. An access accommodation can address a barrier; it cannot substitute for teaching a skill.

Scoring this met is the error to watch for. Scoring it not met for the blanket reason that accommodations never cover needs is the right answer reached by the wrong rule, and the facilitated version should push on that.

## Substantive

**S1. Met.**
Goal 1 states "increasing from 4 of 10 to 6 of 10," and the 4 of 10 traces to the curriculum-based measure named in present levels. Goal 2 states "increasing from 2 of 8 to 6 of 8," tracing to the district problem-solving measure. Each goal carries its own baseline and each baseline is traceable, so the indicator is met.

Compare this against sample A, where the same numbers existed in present levels and never appeared in a goal.

**S2. Not met.**
Goal 1 is otherwise the strongest goal in either sample document. It names its materials, its baseline, its target, its consistency requirement, its schedule, and a tool specific enough to replicate. Then it asks Priya to move from 4 of 10 to 6 of 10 across roughly thirty-six weeks, which is one additional correct item about every eighteen weeks, for a student sitting at the 8th percentile. Present levels explains that her errors concentrate in items where she completes the first step correctly and stops, meaning the skill gap is procedural and addressable. A target this low does not close the gap the document itself describes.

Goal 2, moving 2 of 8 to 6 of 8, is defensible.

**The scope rule decides this one.** S2 is scored across every goal, so goal 2's sound target cannot rescue goal 1's, and the indicator scores not met for the document. A learner who split the difference and marked it partly met has no such option, and the reason matters more than the rule: Priya has a goal that expects almost nothing of her, and averaging it against a better goal hides the child the finding is about.

This is also the clearest demonstration in module 1 that compliance review cannot protect a student. Goal 1 satisfies every compliance indicator it touches and fails Endrew F. on the arithmetic.

**S3. Met.**
Goal 1 names a two-step equation reference card. Goal 2 names a four-step problem-solving graphic organizer. Both state what Priya has access to while working.

**S4. Met.**
Goal 1's tool is a 10-item two-step equation probe, scored for correct final answers, administered at independent practice level. Goal 2's is an 8-item district problem-solving measure, scored with the district four-point work-shown rubric, administered during math class. Each names its contents, its scoring, and its setting, so two teachers would administer them the same way.

**S5. Met.**
A teacher who has never met Priya could read these two sections and teach both goals on Monday, with the materials named, the baselines stated, and the measures replicable.

This indicator asks whether the sections as written are teachable, not whether the document is complete. The missing reading goal is a real failure and C5 is where it scores. Marking S5 not met for the same reason double-counts one problem, which is why the indicators are separated.

## Where learners go wrong on this document

Three errors account for most of the disagreement, and each is worth naming in the debrief.

Scoring C1 met because the math data is thorough, which misses that the indicator applies to every area of need described.

Averaging S2 across the two goals, which the instrument does not permit and which conceals the weaker goal.

Marking S5 not met on the strength of the missing reading goal, which is C5's finding rather than S5's.
