## Internal design note, not for learners

Fully synthetic. No real student, family, district, or document contributed any part of this, so nothing here carries a FERPA obligation and it can be distributed without redaction review.

This document exists to teach the module's central claim in its purest form: **every compliance condition is met and every substantive condition fails.** Compliance 5, substantive 0, total 5 of 10.

That is what makes it worth walking through slowly. Nothing here is missing. A monitoring visit would find a named and dated assessment with a score, a disability-effect statement, observable verbs, criteria, stated measurement methods, a reporting schedule, and a goal for every need. The document is complete, and it still tells a new teacher almost nothing about what to do on Monday.

| Condition | Score | How it lands |
|---|---|---|
| C1, named assessment, date, score, each area of need | Met | R-CBM and the district writing rubric, both dated, both scored |
| C2, disability effect on the general curriculum | Met | Names how decoding load affects this student in this curriculum |
| C3, observable skill and criterion, every goal | Met | Read, write, and answer, each with a criterion |
| C4, method and reporting schedule, every goal | Met | Every goal names a method and the quarterly schedule |
| C5, every present-levels need has a goal | Met | Fluency, comprehension, and written expression each have one |
| S1, quantified baseline in the goal, every goal | Not met | Present levels holds the numbers, no goal carries one |
| S2, appropriately ambitious rate of improvement | Not met | 62 to 75 words correct per minute across a year |
| S3, condition, setting, or materials, every goal | Not met | Absent from all three goals |
| S4, administrable measurement tool, every goal | Not met | Classroom reading assessments, writing samples, teacher-created questions |
| S5, passes the stranger test as a set | Not met | The parts are all present and do not cohere into a plan |

**The two judgments to walk through slowly.**

S1 against C1. Present levels reports 62 words correct per minute, so C1 is met. No goal states that number, so S1 fails. A learner will want to give credit because the baseline exists somewhere in the document, and the reason it fails is that a goal has to carry its own trajectory: a teacher reading the goal alone cannot see whether the year expects meaningful growth. This distinction is the single most useful thing module 1 teaches, and it is invisible to any compliance review.

S2 as its own finding rather than a consequence of S1. Present levels gives 62 words correct per minute and goal 1 targets 75 by year end, which is roughly 0.36 words per week across thirty-six weeks. Typical fifth-grade growth on the same measure runs near a word per week. The goal therefore asks for about a third of ordinary growth from a student already well behind, so it fails the Endrew F. standard on the arithmetic, and the learner should compute it rather than sense it.

Prose quality inside the document below is deliberately hollow in the specific ways the module teaches learners to recognize. Everything required is present, which is the point.

## Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance

**Student strengths.** Marcus contributes readily to class discussion, retains information presented orally, and has strong general knowledge in science and social studies. He works productively in pairs and asks for help when a task is clear.

**Parent input.** Marcus's family reports that reading assignments take roughly three times as long at home as his teachers estimate, and that he avoids independent reading. They would like him reading closer to his grade placement by the end of the year.

**Academic achievement, reading.** On the aimsweb Reading Curriculum-Based Measure administered September 12, 2026, Marcus read 62 words correct per minute with 94% accuracy on the fifth-grade passage set, placing him at the 9th percentile. The fall benchmark for fifth grade is 110 words correct per minute. On the same date, Marcus answered 5 of 10 literal and inferential comprehension questions correctly on the accompanying passage set.

**Academic achievement, written expression.** On the district writing rubric administered September 15, 2026, Marcus scored 2 of 4 in organization and 2 of 4 in conventions on an on-demand narrative prompt. His writing averaged 41 words per sample across three samples, against a fifth-grade expectation of approximately 90.

**Functional performance.** Marcus navigates the building independently, follows classroom routines, and manages his materials without support. No functional concerns are identified.

**Effect of disability.** Marcus's specific learning disability in basic reading skills affects his progress in the general education curriculum in every subject that requires him to read independently. Because decoding consumes most of his attention, he retains less of what he reads than what he hears, so he performs substantially better in discussion-based tasks than on the same content in print. In writing, the same decoding load limits his ability to reread and revise his own drafts.

## Measurable Annual Goals

**Goal 1, Reading Fluency.** By October 2027, Marcus will read fifth-grade passages at 75 words correct per minute, as measured by classroom reading assessments. Progress will be reported quarterly on the district progress report form.

**Goal 2, Reading Comprehension.** By October 2027, Marcus will answer literal and inferential comprehension questions about fifth-grade text with 70% accuracy, as measured by teacher-created comprehension questions. Progress will be reported quarterly on the district progress report form.

**Goal 3, Written Expression.** By October 2027, Marcus will write an organized narrative paragraph scoring 3 of 4 in organization on the district writing rubric, as measured by classroom writing samples. Progress will be reported quarterly on the district progress report form.
