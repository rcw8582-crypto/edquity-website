The manifestation meeting ends, the removal stands, and the assistant principal mentions the district's alternative school as though the destination were settled weeks ago. One sentence of federal regulation says otherwise, and that sentence puts you at the table.

## The whole regulation is one sentence

Section 300.531 provides, in full, that the child's IEP team determines the interim alternative educational setting for services under § 300.530(c), (d)(5), and (g). Those three cross-references cover the removals that matter most: removals extending beyond 10 consecutive school days after the team finds the behavior was not a manifestation of the disability (§ 300.530(c)), removals that amount to a change of placement under § 300.536 (§ 300.530(d)(5)), and the special circumstances removals of up to 45 school days for weapons, illegal drugs or controlled substances, or serious bodily injury (§ 300.530(g)).

You sit on that team. Under 34 C.F.R. § 300.321(a)(1), the parents of the child are members of the IEP team, so the decision about where your child learns during a removal belongs to a group that includes you rather than to an administrator acting alone.

## Why the team decision matters

Districts often operate a single alternative setting and route every removed student there by default, and a default is an administrative habit rather than a team decision. The distinction matters because the setting must do real work: under § 300.530(d)(1)(i), your child must continue to receive educational services in that setting so they can keep participating in the general education curriculum and keep progressing toward the goals in the IEP. A setting the team selects with your child's actual services and goals in front of it looks different from a setting selected by routine.

If the district announces a destination without convening the team, ask in writing when the IEP team will meet to determine the interim alternative educational setting under § 300.531. Naming the regulation signals that you are requesting a step the district already owes rather than a favor.

> The child's IEP Team determines the interim alternative educational setting for services under § 300.530(c), (d)(5), and (g).
>
> 34 C.F.R. § 300.531

## What to ask when the team meets

Ask how each service in the IEP continues in the proposed setting, including related services such as speech or counseling, since the removal changes the location rather than the district's obligations. Ask how the school will measure and report progress toward IEP goals while your child is there, because a removal period without data becomes a gap no one can account for, and our post on [reading progress reports](/news/how-to-read-iep-progress-reports) explains what those reports owe you. Ask how long the removal lasts in school days, with a specific return date, since every count in these regulations runs in school days rather than calendar days. Ask what the return will look like: who meets, what changes, and what support will keep the behavior from recurring.

Leave the meeting with the setting, the services, the duration in school days, and the return date documented in writing. A team decision no one wrote down behaves like a unilateral one when memories differ later.
